What the ads buy, month by month

Blended, not attributed. There is no click-id or UTM join between a Meta ad and a quiz submission in this warehouse, so these divide all Meta spend by all quiz activity that month — including leads that came from email, organic or direct. It is a fair read of what we paid against what the business got, and it is not Meta's own cost per lead (that is the "Meta reports" column, shown beside it so the two can be compared rather than confused). Months where ads ran less than half the month are greyed: a partial flight divided by a whole month of leads is not a rate.

Where the funnel leaks

reach → leads, per ad

Read each row left to right as a chain: of the people who got this far, what share went one step further? Reached → saw it → clicked → page loaded → became a lead. A weak step shows up as a pale cell, instead of having to be inferred from five separate totals.
“… page loaded” is the step nobody measures. Someone clicks the ad — you have already paid for that click — but the page never finishes loading, so they never arrive. A slow page, a bad redirect, a broken link. Meta still bills the click, and it is invisible in any report that stops at CTR.
Shading compares each column against itself: a 1.8% click rate and a 15% lead rate cannot share a scale, so darker only ever means "better than the other ads at this step". Reach is a daily sum, not unique people — Meta de-duplicates only inside a window it is asked for, and these rows are per-ad-per-day, so someone reached on three days counts three times. Rates compare fairly between ads; don't read the reach column as a headcount. Ads too thin to judge on a rate are dimmed.
